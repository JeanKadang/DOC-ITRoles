# Skills progression framework

This document defines the standard career levels used across all domains in this repository and provides domain-by-domain progression ladders. Use it to understand what is expected at each level, how to build toward the next level, and which role files map to each step in your career path. The framework applies to technical individual contributor (IC) and people-leadership tracks.

## Standard role levels

| Level | Focus | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer | Execution and learning | Single domain, guided |
| Senior Engineer | Autonomous delivery, mentoring | Single domain, independent |
| Architect | Design and standards | Domain-wide, technical authority |
| Lead Architect | Team leadership, cross-domain standards | Multi-domain cluster |
| Principal Architect | Strategy and vision | Organisation-wide IC |
| Chapter Lead | Chapter ownership, people leadership across a domain cluster | All domains within a chapter |
| Technical Area Lead (TAL) | Technical area leadership, people management | All of IT, technical |
| Product Area Lead (PAL) | Area delivery, budget, people management | All of IT, business |
| CISO | Information security strategy, risk, and compliance | Organisation-wide security posture |
| SVP of Technology | Technology strategy, investment, and organisational leadership | Entire technology function |
| CIO | IT strategy, business systems, and service delivery leadership | Organisation-wide IT and operations |
| CTO | Technology innovation, engineering excellence, and product technology leadership | Organisation-wide technology and engineering |
| CEO | Overall organisational leadership and accountability | Entire organisation |

## Engineer → Senior Engineer progression

Moving from Engineer to Senior Engineer requires demonstrating consistent independent delivery and the beginning of technical influence.

- **Technical depth** — solves complex problems independently; proposes solutions rather than waiting to be told what to build
- **Quality** — consistent delivery with minimal rework; proactively participates in code and design reviews
- **Influence** — mentors junior peers; contributes to team standards, runbooks, and reusable patterns
- **Communication** — explains technical decisions clearly to non-engineers and to stakeholders outside the team

Typical timeline: **3–5 years** (faster with exceptional performance and demonstrated competency)

## Senior Engineer → Architect progression

The Architect level represents a shift from individual delivery to owning technical direction for a domain.

- **Design thinking** — can design full systems and subsystems, not just implement them; articulates trade-offs at scale and over time
- **Standards ownership** — contributes to or owns domain reference architectures, patterns libraries, and technology decisions
- **Stakeholder engagement** — presents to and aligns senior stakeholders on architectural decisions; comfortable in architecture review boards
- **Breadth** — deep expertise in own domain plus working knowledge of at least two adjacent domains
- **Governance** — understands compliance requirements affecting the domain; participates in governance forums

Typical timeline: **3–6 years** as a Senior Engineer

## Architect → Lead/Principal → Chapter Lead progression

Lead Architect and Principal Architect roles require demonstrable impact beyond a single domain.

- **Multi-domain authority** — consistently resolves conflicts across domain boundaries; trusted by peer architects from other domains
- **Strategy contribution** — shapes 1–3 year technical roadmaps; inputs into technology radar and build/buy/partner decisions
- **Team development** — actively develops architects and senior engineers; gives structured feedback and creates growth opportunities
- **Executive presence** — represents the technical function confidently to business leadership; translates technology risk into business language

The **Chapter Lead** level sits above Principal Architect on the people-leadership track. Chapter Leads own a chapter — a cluster of related domains — and are accountable for technical direction, architecture standards, and practitioner development across all domains in their chapter. Moving to Chapter Lead requires:

- Demonstrated line management or formal mentorship of architect-level practitioners
- Technical credibility across multiple domains within the chapter scope (not just one)
- Ability to set and defend chapter-wide standards with TAL/PAL alignment
- Experience running hiring, calibration, and performance processes at architect grade

## GenAI vs classical MLOps: two AI platform tracks

The catalog deliberately contains two distinct AI platform ladders — do not conflate them when planning a career move:

- **Classical MLOps** (`ai_governance/`): `ai_platform_engineer` → `ai_platform_architect`. Focused on the ML lifecycle for predictive models — feature stores, training pipelines, model registries, drift monitoring — under the AI governance umbrella.
- **GenAI platform** (`modern_infrastructure/`): `genai_platform_engineer` → `genai_platform_architect`. Focused on LLM-era platform capabilities — model serving and routing, prompt/RAG infrastructure, vector stores, GPU capacity, and GenAI-specific guardrails.

Engineers can move between the tracks; the platform engineering fundamentals transfer, while the governance depth (classical) and inference-infrastructure depth (GenAI) are the respective specialisations to build.

## Cross-domain mobility paths

The moves below represent well-established lateral and forward transitions across the domains in this repository.

- **DevOps Engineer → Cloud Senior Engineer** — natural transition for engineers with a strong IaC and infrastructure automation focus
- **Cloud Architect → Principal Cloud Architect** — seniority progression for architects with multi-cloud strategic delivery experience
- **Security Engineer → DevSecOps Engineer → Security Architect** — security specialism deepening through pipeline integration toward full architectural authority
- **Data Engineer → AI Governance Engineer → AI Platform Architect** (`ai_governance/`) — the classical-MLOps data-to-AI progression; note this targets the *AI Platform Architect* in `ai_governance/`, not the *GenAI Platform Architect* in `modern_infrastructure/` (see the two-track note above). Engineers aiming at the GenAI track typically route via `platform_engineering_engineer` or `mlops_engineer` → `genai_platform_engineer` → `genai_platform_architect`
- **Network Architect → Cloud Architect** — hybrid and cloud-first transition for network specialists expanding into cloud networking and landing zones
- **Any Architect → TAL** — move onto the people-and-technical-leadership track; requires strong domain credibility plus demonstrated people development
- **Kubernetes Engineer → Platform Engineer → DevOps or Cloud Architect** — platform engineering pathway through container orchestration into broader infrastructure or developer platform ownership
- **Senior Engineer → governance specialist** — experienced senior engineers move into the specialist Senior-Engineer-grade governance roles: `major_incident_manager` and `change_release_manager` (from operations/SRE backgrounds), `technical_program_manager` (from delivery-heavy roles), `vendor_supplier_it_asset_manager` (from platform roles with vendor exposure), `grc_risk_compliance_analyst` (from security engineering), `business_continuity_disaster_recovery_manager` (from data-protection engineering), and `data_governance_lead` / `data_privacy_officer` (from data engineering or security)
- **Storage/backup specialist → BC/DR Manager** — Commvault, SimpliVity, and storage senior engineers are the natural feeder pool for `business_continuity_disaster_recovery_manager`, which adds business-impact analysis and recovery governance to their technical recovery depth
