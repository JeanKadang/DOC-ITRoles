# Chapter Lead — Onboarding Template

> **How to use this template**
> This template is specifically for onboarding a new **Chapter Lead**. It replaces the generic onboarding template for this role. The manager (TAL or PAL) should complete the "Before day 1" checklist before the new hire's first day. The new Chapter Lead and their manager should review the 30/60/90-day goals together on day 1 and use them as the basis for structured check-ins. The Chapter Lead-specific onboarding checklist at the end of this document maps directly to the Chapter Lead role responsibilities — treat it as a concrete accountability tracker, not a courtesy list. Adapt deadlines where a start date falls across a public holiday period or organisational freeze.

## 30-day goals — Learn

The first 30 days are for orientation. The new Chapter Lead should listen, read, and ask questions. They should not yet attempt to change standards, chair governance sessions, or redirect team priorities.

### Organisational orientation

- [ ] Complete company induction (HR, security, and compliance)
- [ ] Meet all direct reports (domain architects and senior engineers across the chapter) — at least one 1:1 per person by day 14
- [ ] Meet all peer Chapter Leads — at least one 1:1 per Chapter Lead by day 30
- [ ] Meet the Enterprise Architect and understand the EA governance process, including how chapter decisions interact with enterprise-level architecture
- [ ] Meet TAL/PAL and agree on first 90-day priorities, any burning platform issues, and governance expectations
- [ ] Attend at least one cross-chapter governance forum or enterprise architecture forum as an observer
- [ ] Understand the team's operating rhythm: chapter architecture review board cadence, 1:1 schedule, team meetings, and any standing cross-domain forums

### Technical and domain orientation

- [ ] Read the Chapter Lead role definition in full — map each responsibility against the chapter's current state and identify where the chapter is mature vs. where there are gaps
- [ ] Review all domain role definitions in your chapter — understand scope, seniority levels, and the current practitioners assigned to each domain
- [ ] Review existing chapter architecture standards, technology radar, and any active Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) across all chapter domains
- [ ] Attend and observe (do not chair) the chapter architecture review board in your first two weeks — understand the current decision-making process, quality bar, and recurring themes
- [ ] Review the chapter roadmap, backlog, and any outstanding architecture or delivery commitments already in flight
- [ ] Review [Career Paths & Skills Progression](SKILLS_PROGRESSION.md) and [Domain Interactions](CROSS_DOMAIN_INTERACTIONS.md) with specific focus on your chapter's domains — understand how your domains interact with other chapters
- [ ] Review any active platform consolidation or rationalisation programmes affecting your chapter

### Stakeholder and context orientation

- [ ] Understand how the chapter interfaces with finance and procurement — identify any active vendor relationships, contracts, or budget cycles relevant to the chapter
- [ ] Understand the CISO's expectations for the chapter — any security standards, compliance obligations, or outstanding security architecture work
- [ ] Understand how hiring decisions are made in the chapter — existing vacancies, pipeline status, and approval process for new roles

### 30-day check-in

Discuss the following with your manager (TAL/PAL) at the 30-day check-in:

1. What is the current health of the chapter — people, standards, and roadmap?
2. Where are the biggest gaps between the chapter's current state and where it needs to be?
3. What are the top 3 priorities you have identified for your first 90 days?
4. Are there any blockers to your effectiveness that the TAL/PAL can help resolve?
5. How is the working relationship with each direct report developing? Any early concerns?

## 60-day goals — Contribute

By day 60, the Chapter Lead should be actively leading — not just observing. They should be running their own governance sessions, forming their own view of the chapter's priorities, and contributing tangible artefacts.

### Delivery and governance

- [ ] Chair your first chapter architecture review board session — take ownership of the agenda, facilitation, and decision recording
- [ ] Deliver at least one meaningful chapter artefact: an updated architecture standard, a new ADR, or a chapter roadmap update
- [ ] Identify the top 3 cross-domain priorities for the chapter — technical debt, standards gaps, or capability improvements — and document them with a proposed approach
- [ ] Raise at least one improvement or risk identified during the learn phase with TAL/PAL — do not wait for 90 days to surface concerns
- [ ] Identify the top 2 cross-chapter dependencies affecting your chapter and initiate conversations with peer Chapter Leads to agree a resolution approach

### People and chapter

- [ ] Complete talent assessment: for each direct report, assess current performance, development needs, and career trajectory; document findings in the chapter career development framework
- [ ] Calibrate the chapter's hiring bar — define clearly what "good" looks like for architect and senior engineer roles in your chapter; socialise this with TAL/PAL and peer Chapter Leads
- [ ] Establish chapter governance cadence: confirm the chapter architecture review board schedule, 1:1 rhythm, and chapter team meeting frequency — document this so it is visible to all chapter members

### Standards and tooling

- [ ] Begin contributing to or refreshing the chapter technology radar — facilitate at least one Adopt / Trial / Hold / Retire discussion with the chapter's domain architects
- [ ] Become proficient with LeanIX or equivalent EA tooling — ensure chapter architecture is accurately represented
- [ ] Understand the incident response process for your chapter's platform domains and confirm your direct reports understand their escalation paths

### Relationships

- [ ] Have a working relationship established with each peer Chapter Lead — know their chapter's current priorities and where your chapter's work intersects with theirs
- [ ] Have introduced yourself and established a cadence with the Enterprise Architect
- [ ] Have a confirmed reporting and governance rhythm with TAL/PAL

### 60-day check-in

Discuss the following with your manager (TAL/PAL) at the 60-day check-in:

1. What chapter artefact(s) have you delivered, and what was the outcome?
2. What is the outcome of the talent assessment — are there direct reports who need immediate support or who are ready for promotion?
3. Are the top 3 cross-domain priorities aligned with what TAL/PAL expected? Any surprises?
4. How is the chapter architecture review board running — what changes have you made or plan to make?
5. Are there cross-chapter dependencies or risks that need TAL/PAL escalation?

## 90-day goals — Own

By day 90, the Chapter Lead should be fully operational: owning the chapter roadmap, running governance independently, and representing the chapter without supervision.

### Delivery and chapter ownership

- [ ] Own and deliver the chapter roadmap update — present to TAL/PAL with priorities, dependencies, and resource requirements clearly articulated
- [ ] Have delivered at least one cross-domain improvement: a resolved dependency, a consolidated platform component, a new standard, or a capability uplift that spans two or more domains
- [ ] Have represented the chapter independently at an enterprise architecture forum or cross-chapter governance forum — not just as an observer
- [ ] Have contributed meaningfully to at least one enterprise-wide architecture or technology investment discussion

### People and career development

- [ ] Have agreed personal development plans documented for all direct reports
- [ ] Have completed at least one formal development review conversation with each direct report
- [ ] Have been involved in at least one technical interview for a chapter vacancy, if vacancies exist — and confirmed your chapter's hiring bar is documented
- [ ] Have presented chapter people health to TAL/PAL: retention risks, capability gaps, and any talent concerns

### Standards and governance

- [ ] Updated chapter technology radar with full input from all domain architects — reflect the chapter's current and intended direction
- [ ] Produced at least one ADR or architecture standard update that is published and communicated to the chapter
- [ ] Have a clear view of compliance and security architecture obligations for the chapter — any outstanding items have owners and timelines

### Growth

- [ ] Have a personal development plan agreed with TAL/PAL — including any certification targets aligned to role expectations
- [ ] Have identified one area of the chapter or chapter Lead role where you want to deepen expertise in the next 6 months
- [ ] Have enrolled in or completed at least one learning activity from the suggested resources table

### 90-day review

Discuss the following with your manager (TAL/PAL) at the 90-day review:

1. What chapter roadmap artefact did you deliver, and how was it received by TAL/PAL and the chapter?
2. What cross-domain improvement did you deliver — what was the measurable impact?
3. How is the chapter performing against its KPIs: retention, hiring velocity, architecture standards adoption, review board throughput?
4. What is the status of each direct report's personal development plan?
5. What is your own personal development focus for the next 6 months?
6. What is the single biggest risk to the chapter's success in the next quarter, and what is the plan to address it?

## Chapter Lead-specific onboarding checklist

These tasks are specific to the Chapter Lead role and should be completed within the first 90 days. They are drawn directly from the Chapter Lead role responsibilities and are designed to be tracked independently of the 30/60/90-day narrative goals above. Use this as a concrete accountability tracker at each check-in.

### People and chapter

- [ ] Complete 1:1s with all direct reports (domain architects and senior engineers) within the first 2 weeks (by day 14)
- [ ] Document each direct report's current development goals and any performance context from the prior manager (by day 30)
- [ ] Agree personal development plan with each direct report (by day 90)
- [ ] Calibrate your chapter's hiring bar: define what "good" looks like for architect and senior engineer roles in your chapter (by day 60)
- [ ] Participate in or lead at least one technical interview for a chapter vacancy (by day 90, if vacancies exist)

### Architecture governance

- [ ] Read all existing chapter architecture standards and identify any gaps or outdated standards (by day 30)
- [ ] Chair your first chapter architecture review board (by day 60)
- [ ] Produce at least one ADR or architecture standard update (by day 90)
- [ ] Refresh or validate the chapter technology radar with input from all domain architects (by day 90)

### Chapter strategy and roadmap

- [ ] Review the current chapter roadmap and backlog (by day 30)
- [ ] Identify top 3 cross-domain priorities and present to TAL/PAL (by day 60)
- [ ] Own and deliver the first chapter roadmap update (by day 90)

### Cross-chapter relationships

- [ ] Complete 1:1s with all peer Chapter Leads (by day 30)
- [ ] Attend enterprise architecture forum as an observer (by day 30)
- [ ] Present chapter interests independently at an enterprise architecture or cross-chapter forum (by day 90)
- [ ] Identify the top 2 cross-chapter dependencies affecting your chapter and agree resolution approach with peer Chapter Leads (by day 60)

### TAL/PAL alignment

- [ ] Agree 90-day priorities with TAL/PAL (by day 7)
- [ ] Confirm chapter reporting cadence and governance expectations (by day 14)
- [ ] Present chapter health assessment (people, standards, roadmap) to TAL/PAL (by day 90)
