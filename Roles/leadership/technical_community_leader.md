## Role Overview

The Technical Community Leader is a senior individual contributor responsible for building and sustaining the technical community across the engineering organisation. This role drives technical excellence not through line management authority but through influence, curation, and connection — establishing and running communities of practice (CoPs), facilitating knowledge sharing across domain boundaries, maintaining shared standards libraries, and operating the organisation's technology radar. The Technical Community Leader ensures that hard-won technical knowledge does not remain siloed within individual teams, that practitioners across all domains have forums for peer learning, and that engineering standards evolve through collective input rather than top-down mandate.

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Accelerates engineering capability growth and reduces duplicated effort across the organisation by creating structures and habits for technical knowledge sharing, standards reuse, and cross-domain collaboration — directly improving delivery velocity, quality consistency, and engineer retention
- **Value Metrics:** CoP attendance and active participation rates, cross-domain knowledge transfer events per quarter, internal technical content published, practitioner satisfaction score, adoption rate of shared standards and patterns
- **Key Stakeholders:** All domain Architects, Technical Area Leads (TALs), Engineering Practices Champion, HR/L&D function, and engineering teams across all domains
- **Processes Supported:** Community of practice governance, internal technology radar, engineering standards lifecycle, technical knowledge management, cross-domain peer review, internal learning and development alignment

## Key Responsibilities

- Establish, run, and continuously improve domain and cross-domain communities of practice, ensuring they deliver genuine value to practitioners
- Maintain and publish the organisation's internal technology radar, coordinating input from domain architects and senior engineers to reflect real adoption signals
- Organise and facilitate internal tech talks, knowledge shares, hackathons, and engineering open days that promote cross-pollination of ideas
- Own the engineering standards library — curating, versioning, and communicating approved patterns, templates, and reference architectures in accessible, maintained repositories
- Publish and maintain internal technical content (newsletters, wikis, blog posts, how-to guides) that keeps the practitioner community informed and connected
- Facilitate cross-domain peer reviews, encouraging teams to share designs, receive external challenge, and contribute to collective learning
- Work closely with HR/L&D to align community learning activities with formal training pathways, career frameworks, and skill gap analysis
- Partner with the Architecture function to ensure community-driven standards and patterns align with enterprise architectural direction
- Identify emerging knowledge gaps across the organisation and design targeted community interventions — workshops, working groups, or learning sprints — to address them
- Champion psychological safety and inclusive participation within technical communities, ensuring all voices contribute to shared standards and practices

## Key Decisions and Accountabilities

> Clarifies what this role **owns** (decides independently) vs. **advises on** (input without final authority).

| Owns | Advises On |
|---|---|
| Community of practice structure, cadence, format, and facilitation approach | Enterprise architecture policy and domain-level technical direction (Architecture function and TAL-owned) |
| Technology radar content, categorisation, and publication cadence | Technology investment and procurement decisions (TAL and business stakeholders-owned) |
| Engineering standards library structure, versioning, and contribution process | Mandatory standards and architectural constraints for specific domains (Domain Architect-owned) |
| Internal technical content calendar, format, and publication channels | Engineering headcount, team structure, and L&D budget allocation (PAL/HR-owned) |
| Cross-domain peer review process and participation guidelines | Individual team delivery priorities and sprint planning (Delivery Lead and team-owned) |

## Required Skills

- Demonstrated ability to facilitate technical communities, workshops, and peer forums with diverse practitioner audiences — combining structure with genuine curiosity and energy
- Strong written and verbal communication skills, with the ability to distil complex technical topics into accessible newsletters, wiki articles, and reference guides for varied audiences
- Broad technical literacy across multiple engineering domains (cloud, software, data, platform, security) sufficient to curate credible cross-domain content and facilitate meaningful technical discussion
- Experience running or contributing to technology radar processes, engineering standards programmes, or internal knowledge management systems
- Facilitation and workshop design skills, including the ability to run remote and hybrid sessions using collaborative tools effectively
- Ability to build trust and engagement with senior engineers and architects across the organisation without formal authority — influence through expertise, consistency, and service
- Understanding of adult learning principles and how formal L&D and informal community learning complement each other
- Experience with knowledge management platforms (Confluence, SharePoint, Backstage TechDocs) and open standards repositories (GitHub)

**Technology Proficiency Levels:**

- **Expert level required:** GitHub/GitLab community and repository features, Confluence/SharePoint knowledge base authoring
- **Proficient level required:** Learning management platforms (Degreed, Cornerstone, internal LMS), Miro/Mural collaborative facilitation
- **Working Knowledge required:** Metrics and analytics tooling (survey platforms, community engagement dashboards)
- **Awareness level expected:** AI-powered learning and content tools, community platform analytics

## Qualifications

- **Education:** Degree in Computer Science, Software Engineering, or a related technical discipline, or equivalent professional experience
- **Experience:** Typically 7+ years in software or platform engineering roles, with at least 2–3 years of demonstrated leadership, mentoring, or community-building activity; experience at Senior Engineer or Architect level in at least one domain
- **Certifications:** No mandatory certifications required for this role; recommended certifications listed in the Certifications section below

## Key Technologies

- Confluence — primary platform for community wikis, standards documentation, and internal knowledge base
- SharePoint — secondary content and document management for organisation-wide communications
- Backstage (TechDocs) — developer portal and golden path documentation; standards and template publishing
- GitHub — standards repositories, contribution workflows, and version-controlled reference architectures
- Microsoft Teams — community channel management, CoP meeting hosting, internal tech talk streaming
- Miro — remote and hybrid workshop facilitation, technology radar boards, and collaborative session design
- Internal ITSM and portfolio tools — for tracking community initiatives and standards adoption
- Survey and feedback tooling (Microsoft Forms, Mentimeter, or equivalent) — for community engagement measurement and practitioner satisfaction tracking

## Typical Day-to-Day Activities

- Preparing and facilitating a community of practice session — setting the agenda, sourcing speakers, hosting discussions, and capturing and publishing outcomes
- Reviewing and updating technology radar entries based on input gathered from domain architects and senior engineers
- Writing or editing an internal technical newsletter or wiki article to share recent learnings, community highlights, or newly approved standards
- Meeting with a domain architect to understand an emerging pattern or standard and work out how to communicate it across the wider community
- Coordinating with HR/L&D to map upcoming community workshops to skill gaps identified in engineering career framework reviews
- Reviewing submissions to the engineering standards library — providing feedback, suggesting alignment with existing patterns, and publishing approved contributions
- Designing a cross-domain peer review session or hackathon event, including invitations, logistics, tooling setup, and output format
- Responding to practitioner requests for guidance on where to find standards, patterns, or subject matter experts across the organisation
- Analysing community engagement metrics — attendance trends, content views, contribution rates — and iterating the community programme to improve reach and value
- Attending architecture forums or TAL-level governance meetings to ensure community-generated standards remain aligned with enterprise direction

## Key Performance Indicators

- CoP session attendance rate and active participation trend (contributors, not just attendees)
- Number of cross-domain knowledge transfer events facilitated per quarter
- Volume and currency of internal technical content published (articles, guides, radar updates, standards)
- Practitioner satisfaction score from periodic community engagement surveys
- Adoption rate of shared standards and patterns across engineering teams
- Time from standard proposal to published and communicated adoption guidance
- Hackathon and knowledge share event participation across domain boundaries
- HR/L&D alignment coverage — proportion of community learning activities mapped to career framework competencies

## Remote Work Considerations

- **Remote Eligibility:** Highly remote eligible; community building, content creation, and standards curation are well-suited to async-first working patterns
- **Collaboration Tools:** Microsoft Teams, Confluence, Miro, Backstage, GitHub, and SharePoint; strong async-first communication practices are essential for distributed community engagement
- **On-Site Requirements:** Recommended on-site or co-located presence for major community events, hackathons, and cross-domain workshops where in-person energy significantly improves outcomes
- **Time Zone Flexibility:** Standard business hours with flexibility to accommodate engineers across distributed time zones for CoP sessions and knowledge shares
- **On-Call / Operational Demands:** No operational on-call requirement; occasional evening or cross-time-zone availability for community events or external tech talks

## Career Development Path

**Previous Roles:**

- Senior Software Engineer with demonstrated mentoring, documentation, or community contribution history
- Domain Architect (Cloud, Data, Security, or other) seeking a broader org-wide influence role
- Principal or Staff Engineer with cross-domain technical breadth and a history of internal knowledge sharing
- Developer Experience Engineer or Platform Engineer transitioning to community and standards leadership

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Engineering Director (with expanded people leadership and organisational accountability)
- Architecture Lead or Enterprise Architect (with deepened architectural governance scope)
- Technical Area Lead (PAL/TAL pair, with full product area technical ownership)
- Head of Engineering Enablement or Head of Developer Experience

## Recommended Certifications & Learning Paths

**Core Certifications:**

- TOGAF Foundation (The Open Group Architecture Framework — for credibility and shared language with the Architecture function)
- ICP-ACC (ICAgile Certified Professional — Agile Coaching) — for facilitation, coaching, and community leadership skills
- Certified Facilitator (IAF Certified Professional Facilitator or equivalent) — for structured community and workshop facilitation

**Complementary Certifications:**

- SAFe Lean Portfolio Management or SAFe for Architects (for enterprise alignment context)
- AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate or Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (for cross-domain technical credibility)
- Knowledge Management Professional (KMP) or equivalent — for formal knowledge management and community design frameworks
- ICP-ENT (ICAgile — Enterprise Agile Coaching) for broader organisational change enablement

**Learning Resources and Communities:**

- Community Roundtable (communityroundtable.com) — professional community management research and practice, Technology Radar methodology (Thoughtworks) for curating and running internal radars, staffeng.com for IC leadership and influence patterns at senior levels, IASA Global and The Open Group for architecture community engagement, and internal architecture and engineering communities of practice as both participant and facilitator
