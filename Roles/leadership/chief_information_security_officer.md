## Role Overview

The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is the senior executive accountable for the organisation's information security strategy, risk framework, regulatory compliance posture, and overall security capability. The CISO owns the strategic direction of all security disciplines — including cyber defence, identity and access management, security operations, data protection, and third-party risk — and holds executive accountability for the organisation's security posture at board level.

In many organisations the CISO reports into the SVP of Technology (or equivalent CTO/CIO), forming a close executive partnership on technology risk and investment. In some organisations — particularly those with independent security governance or heavily regulated operating environments — the CISO reports directly to the CEO or Board. Both models are valid and reflected in this definition. In some organisations, the SVP of Technology and the CISO role may be held by the same person; the CISO responsibilities are documented separately here for clarity and for organisations where the roles are distinct.

The CISO sits above the PAL and TAL in the technology leadership hierarchy on matters of security governance and risk, and provides strategic direction to the Security & Identity Chapter Lead and senior security architects who deliver operational security.

## Role Scope & Boundaries

- **Scope of Influence:** Organisation-wide — information security strategy, risk framework, and security architecture direction
- **Experience Anchor:** 15+ years of security leadership, 5+ years at CISO or senior security executive level — operates independently on security strategy and risk acceptance
- **Out of Scope:** Overall technology strategy and platform investment priorities (SVP of Technology-owned); total technology budget authority and enterprise-wide financial governance (SVP of Technology and CFO-owned); corporate legal strategy and regulatory engagement beyond security obligations (Legal and CEO-owned)
- **Escalates To:** SVP of Technology (or CEO/Board directly, in independent governance models) for enterprise risk acceptance and major security investment
- **Escalated To By:** Security & Identity Chapter Lead and senior security architects on unresolved security risk, incident escalation, and architecture decisions requiring executive risk acceptance

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures the organisation maintains an appropriate security posture to protect business operations, customer data, and regulatory standing — enabling the business to operate with confidence, meet compliance obligations, and manage cyber risk as a strategic enterprise risk
- **Value Metrics:** Security incident frequency and impact trend, regulatory audit outcomes and compliance posture, mean time to detect and respond to security incidents, third-party risk exposure, board confidence in security governance, security investment efficiency
- **Key Stakeholders:** SVP of Technology (or CEO/Board in independent governance models), Board Risk or Audit Committee, CEO, CFO, COO, Legal and Data Protection Officers, Product Area Leads, Technical Area Lead, Security & Identity Chapter Lead, senior security architects, and regulatory bodies
- **Processes Supported:** Information security strategy and risk management, regulatory compliance and audit management, security incident escalation and crisis management, third-party and supply chain security governance, board risk committee reporting, security investment planning and budgeting, security culture and awareness programmes

## Key Responsibilities

- Own and maintain the enterprise information security strategy, risk framework, and security architecture direction, aligned to the organisation's risk appetite and regulatory obligations
- Hold regulatory and compliance accountability across all applicable frameworks and standards, including ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF, IEC 62443, GDPR, NIS2, SOC 2, DORA, and sector-specific requirements
- Act as the primary escalation point for major security incidents, leading executive response, board communication, and post-incident remediation governance
- Own the security investment budget, prioritising capability and tooling investment against the organisation's risk profile and threat landscape
- Provide strategic direction and executive sponsorship to the Security & Identity Chapter Lead, ensuring the operational security programme is aligned to strategic objectives
- Manage third-party and supply chain security risk oversight, including vendor security assessments, contractual security requirements, and ongoing risk monitoring
- Sponsor and drive security culture and awareness programmes across the organisation, ensuring security is embedded in ways of working at all levels
- Represent the organisation's cyber risk posture at the board risk committee and executive leadership team, translating technical risk into business impact language
- Engage with regulators, auditors, and industry security bodies as the executive accountable for the organisation's security obligations
- Collaborate with the SVP of Technology and CTO/CIO on technology investment decisions where security risk, compliance, or architectural security principles are at stake

## Key Decisions & Accountabilities

## Required Skills & Qualifications

**Technical Skills:**

- Deep expertise in information security across multiple domains: cyber defence, identity and access management, cloud security, data protection, application security, and security operations
- Strong understanding of regulatory and compliance frameworks applicable to the organisation — including ISO 27001, GDPR, NIS2, SOC 2, DORA, PCI DSS, and sector-specific requirements
- Experience owning security architecture direction at enterprise scale, including zero trust, secure-by-design principles, and security reference architectures
- Proven ability to own and manage a security risk register at board level, translating technical vulnerability into business risk language
- Understanding of threat intelligence, security operations centre (SOC) functions, and incident response at strategic level
- Familiarity with cloud security posture management, DevSecOps, and security tooling ecosystems across major cloud platforms

**Leadership and Executive Management:**

- Track record as a senior security leader (CISO, Head of Security, VP of Information Security, or equivalent) with board-level accountability
- Executive communication skills — able to present cyber risk clearly and credibly to non-technical board members, regulators, and business executives
- Experience managing and developing senior security professionals, including security architects and chapter or domain leads
- Strong influencing skills to embed security requirements into technology investment decisions, programme governance, and third-party relationships
- Resilience and decisiveness in high-pressure security incident scenarios and regulatory engagements

### Qualifications

- Degree in Computer Science, Information Security, Information Technology, or a related field; postgraduate qualification advantageous
- 15+ years of progressive security leadership experience, with at least 5 years in a senior executive or CISO-level role
- Demonstrable board-level reporting experience and regulatory engagement track record
- Professional security certifications at senior level (see Recommended Certifications below)

## Typical Day-to-Day Activities

- Reviewing the security risk register, threat intelligence briefings, and active incident status with the Security & Identity Chapter Lead
- Engaging with the SVP of Technology on security investment priorities, risk posture, and technology programme security requirements
- Preparing and presenting cyber risk updates, compliance status reports, and security programme progress for board risk committee and executive leadership
- Overseeing regulatory compliance activities — audit preparation, assessments, and management of findings and remediation commitments
- Reviewing significant third-party security risk assessments and making risk acceptance or escalation decisions
- Participating in major security incident response at the executive level, including stakeholder communication and board notification
- Conducting strategic 1:1s with the Security & Identity Chapter Lead and senior security architects
- Reviewing security architecture proposals for major technology investments, platform changes, and transformation programmes
- Engaging with external security advisors, regulators, and industry bodies on threat landscape and best practice
- Driving the security culture and awareness agenda — reviewing programme effectiveness, sponsoring initiatives, and setting the tone from the top

## Remote Work Considerations

- **Remote Eligibility:** Hybrid; significant in-person presence required for board and executive committee engagement, sensitive regulatory discussions, and major incident response leadership
- **Collaboration Tools:** Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, GRC platforms, SIEM dashboards, and executive reporting tools; secure communication channels for sensitive incident and risk management discussions
- **On-Site Requirements:** On-site for board risk committee meetings, regulatory engagements, major incident command, and key executive forums; travel to key vendor and regulatory locations as required
- **Time Zone Flexibility:** Standard business hours with availability for cross-regional security governance, regulatory engagements across time zones, and major incident escalation at any hour
- **On-Call / Operational Demands:** On call for major security incidents (P1 and critical severity) requiring executive decision-making, regulatory notification assessment, or board escalation; works with the Security & Identity Chapter Lead for operational incident triage

## Career Development Path

**Previous Roles:**

- Head of Information Security or VP of Information Security with board-facing accountability
- Security & Identity Chapter Lead or Head of Security Architecture stepping up to executive scope
- Senior Security Architect or Principal Security Engineer with executive development track record
- CISO in a smaller or less complex organisation, stepping up in scale or sector complexity
- Big Four or specialist security advisory partner transitioning into an executive in-house role

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Group CISO (larger enterprise or group holding company with multiple regulated entities)
- SVP of Technology or CTO/CIO (where the CISO and technology leadership roles are combined)
- Non-executive Director with a cyber and technology risk specialism
- Chief Risk Officer (for organisations where technology and enterprise risk are converging)
- Security technology advisory, board advisory, or private equity portfolio CISO role

## Recommended Certifications & Learning Paths

**Core Certifications:**

- Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) — ISACA (governance and risk management depth)
- Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) — (ISC)² (technical and strategic security breadth)
- ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer or Lead Auditor (for compliance framework depth)

**Complementary Certifications:**

- Certified in Risk and Information Systems Control (CRISC) — ISACA (for risk management depth)
- GDPR Practitioner or Data Protection Officer certification (for data protection regulatory depth)
- SABSA Chartered Security Architect (SCF/SCM) — for enterprise security architecture depth
- Board-level cyber governance programmes (e.g., NCSC Board Toolkit, NACD Cyber Risk Oversight Certificate, or equivalent)
- Executive leadership programmes (MIT Sloan CISO Executive Programme, SANS CISO Network, or equivalent)

**Learning Resources and Communities:**

- (ISC)² and ISACA professional communities and research publications, NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) guidance and threat intelligence, ENISA (European Union Agency for Cybersecurity) regulatory and compliance resources, SANS Institute and CISA advisories, peer networks such as the CISO Forum, FS-ISAC (financial sector), and sector-specific ISACs, and Gartner Security and Risk Management Summit research
