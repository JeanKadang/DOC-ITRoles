## Role Overview

The Cloud, Platform & Infrastructure Chapter Lead is the most senior technical manager and people leader for the organisation's largest technology chapter, spanning eleven domains across the full infrastructure stack from bare metal to cloud-native — including FinOps & Cloud Economics. This role combines direct line management of architect-level practitioners across Cloud Platforms, Kubernetes, Modern Infrastructure, Virtualisation, Specialised Computing, Server Hardware, Linux and Windows Server OS, Network, and FinOps with authoritative technical direction over the entire infrastructure estate. The Chapter Lead owns the infrastructure technology radar, ensures coherence between cloud and on-premises platforms, governs cloud financial discipline, and is accountable for architecture standards across all eleven domains. Reporting to a Technical Area Lead or Product Area Lead, this role is the definitive "Head of Infrastructure" voice below the TAL layer. FinOps sits within this chapter due to the close relationship between cloud cost governance and infrastructure investment decisions, capacity planning, and direct budget accountability to the PAL and TAL.

## Role Scope & Boundaries

- **Scope of Influence:** Cross-domain, chapter-wide — line management and technical authority across eleven infrastructure domains
- **Experience Anchor:** 8+ years in senior technical roles with 2+ years of demonstrated line management or formal mentorship of architect-level practitioners — operates independently across the full chapter scope
- **Out of Scope:** Enterprise-wide architecture policy and cross-chapter technology standards (Enterprise Architect-owned); strategic investment priorities, budget allocation, and programme governance (TAL/PAL-owned); compensation bands, HR policy, and organisational structure (PAL and HR-owned); vendor contracts, procurement decisions, and enterprise supplier strategy (commercial and PAL-owned)
- **Escalates To:** Technical Area Lead (TAL) or Product Area Lead (PAL) — strategic priorities, budget allocation, and decisions beyond chapter-level authority
- **Escalated To By:** Domain Architects and Senior Engineers within the chapter on cross-domain technical conflicts, standards disputes, or decisions requiring chapter-wide authority

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures the organisation's infrastructure estate is technically coherent, strategically directed, and delivered by a high-performing chapter of architects and senior engineers — enabling reliable, scalable, and cost-optimised platforms from bare metal to cloud
- **Value Metrics:** Infrastructure architecture standards adoption rate, cloud vs. on-premises placement decision quality, platform consolidation progress, hardware refresh cycle adherence, chapter practitioner retention and satisfaction, cross-domain dependency resolution speed
- **Key Stakeholders:** Technical Area Lead / Product Area Lead (line manager), domain architects and senior engineers within the chapter, Enterprise Architect, CISO, Finance and procurement, vendor management, CTO/CIO
- **Processes Supported:** Infrastructure architecture governance, cloud placement and workload migration decisions, capacity planning, hardware lifecycle management, platform consolidation programmes, cross-domain dependency management, infrastructure technology radar, FinOps practice and cloud spend governance

## Key Responsibilities

- Line manage and develop architect-level practitioners across all ten infrastructure chapter domains, including performance management, career development, and chapter hiring
- Own and maintain the chapter career development framework for infrastructure architects and senior engineers, running regular 1:1s, development reviews, and talent calibration
- Lead chapter-level hiring: define role standards, conduct or chair technical interviews, and ensure a consistent bar for all infrastructure practitioner appointments
- Set and govern the infrastructure technology radar, driving the Adopt/Trial/Hold/Retire categorisation of platforms and tooling across all ten domains
- Own infrastructure-wide architecture governance — chairing chapter architecture review boards, establishing design standards, and ensuring consistency across cloud and on-premises domains
- Drive cloud vs. on-premises placement decisions and infrastructure workload strategy in alignment with enterprise architecture, ensuring cost governance through the FinOps domain within the chapter
- Oversee platform consolidation and rationalisation programmes, managing cross-domain dependencies to reduce duplication and technical debt
- Govern hardware refresh strategy across server and specialized computing domains, ensuring lifecycle planning aligns with capacity and technology road maps
- Own the infrastructure chapter roadmap — translating TAL/PAL strategic direction into chapter-level technical priorities and delivery commitments
- Represent the chapter in enterprise-wide architecture forums, security governance bodies, and technology investment discussions
