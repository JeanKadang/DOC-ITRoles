## Role Overview

The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) is the most senior executive in the organisation, accountable to the Board of Directors for the overall performance, strategic direction, and long-term sustainability of the enterprise. The CEO owns corporate vision, strategy, culture, and full P&L, and serves as the primary interface between the Board of Directors and the executive leadership team. Technology is one of several strategic domains the CEO governs — alongside Finance, Operations, People, Legal, and Sales — and the CEO sets the organisational risk appetite within which all functions, including the technology function, operate.

The CEO holds the reporting line for the Chief Technology Officer (CTO), Chief Information Officer (CIO), or equivalent SVP of Technology. In organisations where a single senior technology executive exists (for example, an SVP of Technology or a combined CTO/CIO), that person is the primary technology accountability point to the CEO. In organisations with both a CTO and a CIO, the CEO is responsible for maintaining coherence between both reporting lines and ensuring technology strategy and IT operations work in alignment.

In some governance models — particularly in heavily regulated industries or where the Board requires independent security assurance — the CEO also holds the direct CISO reporting line, outside the technology chain of command.

## Role Scope & Boundaries

- **Scope of Influence:** Organisation-wide — full P&L and enterprise capital allocation authority
- **Experience Anchor:** 20+ years of progressive leadership across multiple business functions, 5-10+ years at C-suite or Group level — operates with full autonomy, accountable only to the Board
- **Out of Scope:** Detailed functional strategies and delivery execution (owned by respective C-suite function heads); departmental budget management and functional spend (CFO and function heads-owned); broader people policies and HR frameworks (CPO-owned)
- **Escalates To:** Board of Directors — strategic decisions, major risk issues, executive appointments, or decisions requiring board-level visibility
- **Escalated To By:** CTO / CIO / SVP of Technology, CFO, COO, CISO, and other C-suite peers on enterprise-level risk, investment, or cross-functional conflicts they cannot resolve at their own level

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Leads the organisation toward sustained profitable growth, competitive differentiation, and responsible governance — ensuring all business functions, including technology, finance, operations, and people, operate in alignment with corporate strategy and stakeholder interests
- **Value Metrics:** Revenue growth, EBITDA margin, digital transformation ROI, employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS), customer Net Promoter Score (NPS), regulatory compliance posture, total shareholder return (TSR), and Board confidence
- **Key Stakeholders:** Board of Directors, CFO, COO, CTO, CIO, CISO, Chief People Officer (CPO), Chief Marketing Officer (CMO), shareholders and institutional investors, regulators, and strategic partners
- **Processes Supported:** Corporate strategic planning and annual operating planning, Board governance and reporting, M&A strategy and integration oversight, enterprise risk management, executive talent and succession planning, investor relations, and regulatory and government engagement

## Key Responsibilities

- Own and articulate the corporate vision, strategy, and long-term direction, translating Board expectations into organisational action and executive accountability
- Hold full P&L accountability for the organisation, working with the CFO to manage financial performance, capital allocation, and investment strategy
- Chair or sponsor the executive leadership team, ensuring coherent strategic direction and clear accountability across all business functions
- Set the organisational risk appetite and governance framework within which technology, security, financial, and operational risks are managed and reported
- Report to the Board of Directors on corporate performance, strategic progress, executive appointments, and enterprise risk
- Lead M&A strategy, including target identification, investment rationale, due diligence oversight, and post-merger integration governance at executive level
- Represent the organisation externally — with investors, regulators, government bodies, industry forums, and the media
- Hold the technology reporting line (CTO, CIO, or SVP of Technology) accountable for technology strategy, investment performance, and enterprise technology risk
- Set the cultural and ethical tone for the organisation, sponsoring inclusion, performance, and values-aligned behaviours at all levels
- Drive digital transformation ambition at the enterprise level, ensuring technology investment is positioned as a source of competitive advantage and customer value

## Key Decisions & Accountabilities

> Clarifies what this role **owns** (decides independently) vs. **advises on** (input without final authority).

| Owns | Advises On |
|---|---|
| Corporate vision, strategy, and long-term direction | Detailed functional strategies and delivery execution (owned by respective C-suite function heads) |
| Organisational P&L and enterprise capital allocation | Departmental budget management and functional spend (owned by CFO and function heads) |
| Executive leadership appointments, C-suite organisational design, and succession planning | Broader people policies and HR frameworks (CPO-owned) |
| Enterprise risk appetite and governance framework | Specific risk domain management — technology, security, financial, operational (owned by CTO / CIO / CISO / CFO / COO) |
| Board reporting, investor relations, and shareholder communications | Technical regulatory compliance submissions (owned by Legal and relevant function heads) |
| M&A strategy: target approval, investment rationale, and integration mandate | M&A execution and functional integration workstreams (owned by relevant function heads and integration leads) |
| External representation with regulators, government, and key strategic partners | Technology, security, and operational detail in regulatory engagements (CTO / CIO / CISO / Legal-owned) |

## Required Skills & Qualifications

**Technical Skills:**

- Board-appropriate literacy in technology strategy, digital transformation, and AI — sufficient to hold CTO/CIO/SVP accountable and make informed investment decisions without requiring hands-on technical depth
- Understanding of cyber risk and information security at a governance level — able to translate CISO reporting into enterprise risk decisions and set appropriate risk appetite
- Familiarity with major enterprise technology investment categories: cloud platforms, ERP, data and analytics, and digital product development — sufficient to evaluate business cases and challenge assumptions
- Financial literacy sufficient to govern technology investment ROI, capital allocation, and digital transformation business cases at Board level
- Understanding of regulatory and compliance obligations in the organisation's sector, including technology-related regulations (GDPR, NIS2, DORA, and sector-specific requirements)

**Soft Skills:**

- Visionary leadership — the ability to articulate a compelling and credible future direction and bring the organisation along in conditions of complexity and change
- Executive communication — exceptional at communicating with Boards, investors, regulators, and the media; able to translate complex topics into clear strategic narrative
- Strategic thinking and decisiveness in conditions of ambiguity, competing priorities, and enterprise-wide complexity
- Political and commercial acumen — skilled at managing stakeholder relationships across shareholders, regulators, partners, and industry bodies
- High resilience and composure under pressure, including during crises, major technology incidents, regulatory investigations, and M&A activity
- Collaborative executive leadership style that empowers the C-suite team while maintaining clear accountability and performance standards

### Qualifications

**Education:**

Degree in Business, Economics, Law, Engineering, or a related field. An MBA or other postgraduate qualification from a recognised institution is common at this level and provides a strong foundation for the breadth of commercial and strategic judgement required.

**Certifications:**

| Certification | Body | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate in Company Direction (IoD) | Institute of Directors | Board governance and director accountability |
| NACD Directorship Certification | National Association of Corporate Directors | Corporate governance and board leadership |
| Chartered Director (CDir) | Institute of Directors | Senior governance and strategic leadership |
| Executive leadership programmes (Harvard, INSEAD, London Business School) | Various | Executive strategy and enterprise leadership |

**Experience:**

20+ years of progressive leadership experience across multiple business functions, with at least 5–10 years in C-suite or Group-level roles. Proven track record of P&L ownership, Board-level accountability, and executive leadership across complex, multi-domain organisations.

## Career Development Path

**Previous Roles:**

- COO or CFO with enterprise-wide strategic experience and established Board engagement
- CTO, CIO, or other C-suite leader in a technology-led organisation stepping up to full CEO accountability
- Managing Director or divisional CEO in a subsidiary or large business unit
- SVP or EVP with a proven track record of P&L ownership and executive team leadership
- Management consulting partner with substantial CEO advisory experience transitioning into executive leadership

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Group CEO or Executive Chair in a larger or more complex enterprise
- Non-executive Director or Board Chair across multiple organisations
- Private equity operating partner or portfolio company CEO/Chair
- Government advisory, regulatory, or public sector leadership roles
- Entrepreneurial ventures as founder, executive chairman, or investor

## Interactions with Other Roles

> **Interaction Mode** describes the direction/nature of the relationship: **Collaborates** (peer-to-peer), **Consumes From** (relies on the other role's output/service), **Provides To** (delivers a service the other role consumes), **Governed By** (subject to the other role's standards/approval), or **Escalates To** (routes unresolved issues upward).

| Role | Nature of Interaction | Interaction Mode |
|---|---|---|
| CTO / CIO / SVP of Technology | Direct reporting line; holds technology strategy, investment performance, and enterprise technology risk accountable | Provides To |
| CISO | Direct or indirect reporting line (model-dependent); receives executive cyber risk briefings and sets risk appetite for security governance | Provides To |
| CFO | Joint P&L oversight; capital allocation, investment governance, and Board financial reporting | Collaborates |
| COO | Operational performance accountability; business continuity, enterprise operational risk, and cross-functional delivery | Collaborates |
| CPO (Chief People Officer) | Executive talent strategy, succession planning, organisational design, and culture leadership | Collaborates |
| Board of Directors | Primary governance accountability; strategy, performance, risk, executive appointments, and regulatory posture | Escalates To |
| Investors / Shareholders | External accountability relationship; investor relations, capital markets, and shareholder communications | Escalates To |
| Regulators | External engagement; regulatory posture, formal notifications, government relations, and sector compliance | Escalates To |

## Typical Day-to-Day Activities

- Preparing for and chairing Board meetings, Board committee sessions, and executive leadership team meetings
- Reviewing enterprise performance against the annual operating plan with the CFO and function heads
- Holding strategic 1:1s with C-suite direct reports, including the technology reporting line (CTO/CIO/SVP)
- Engaging investors, analysts, and shareholders through briefings, roadshows, and results communications
- Reviewing enterprise risk posture, including technology and cyber risk briefings from the CISO reporting chain
- Progressing M&A pipeline activity: target reviews, due-diligence checkpoints, and integration steering
- Representing the organisation externally with regulators, government bodies, industry forums, and media
- Driving executive talent, succession, and organisational design decisions with the CPO

## Remote Work Considerations

- **Remote Eligibility:** Hybrid; substantial on-site presence required for Board meetings, executive leadership sessions, investor engagements, and organisational leadership visibility
- **Collaboration Tools:** Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, board governance platforms, and executive dashboards for performance, risk, and strategy reporting
- **On-Site Requirements:** Regular presence at headquarters for Board and executive team cycles; frequent travel for investor relations, regulator engagement, M&A activity, and site leadership visits
- **Time Zone Flexibility:** Extended availability across regions for global investor, Board, and partner engagements
- **On-Call / Operational Demands:** Ultimate escalation point for enterprise crises — major technology or security incidents, regulatory events, and reputational issues — with availability governed by crisis-management protocols

## Common Challenges

- Balancing short-term financial performance demands from shareholders with the long-term investment horizons required for digital transformation and technology capability building
- Maintaining strategic coherence across a complex executive team where technology, operations, people, and finance priorities compete for capital and leadership attention
- Ensuring technology risk — cyber, data, platform resilience — is understood and governed appropriately at Board level without possessing deep technical expertise
- Managing M&A integration complexity when acquiring technology-led businesses, including technology stack rationalisation, cultural integration, and engineering talent retention
- Holding the CTO/CIO/SVP accountable for technology ROI in an environment where technology outcomes are often indirect, lagged, or difficult to attribute to specific investments

## Example Projects

- Sponsoring and governing a multi-year enterprise digital transformation programme, including approval of the technology investment case, executive steering, and Board-level milestone reporting
- Leading M&A activity targeting a technology-native business, including commissioning technology due diligence, shaping the integration strategy, and managing Board approval and investor communications
- Establishing a revised enterprise risk governance framework that elevates cyber and technology risk to the Board Audit and Risk Committee as a standing agenda item
- Driving an enterprise AI strategy — commissioning the executive team to develop a Board-level position on AI adoption, governance model, ethical principles, and competitive use cases
- Managing the organisation's executive response during a major technology or security incident, including Board communication, regulatory notification, external media engagement, and post-incident remediation governance
