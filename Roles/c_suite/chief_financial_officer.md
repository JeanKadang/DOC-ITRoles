## Role Overview

The Chief Financial Officer (CFO) is the senior executive accountable for the organisation's financial strategy, financial reporting, capital allocation, treasury, and financial risk management. The CFO owns the integrity of the financial statements, the organisation's capital structure and funding strategy, and the governance frameworks that ensure financial compliance and accountability across the enterprise. The CFO partners directly with the CEO and Board to translate business strategy into financial plans, investment priorities, and risk-adjusted capital decisions.

In most organisations the CFO reports directly to the CEO and is a peer of the CTO and CIO. The CFO holds formal accountability to the Board's Audit and Risk Committee, acts as the primary contact for external auditors and investors, and — in publicly listed organisations — leads investor relations and financial disclosure obligations.

The CFO's remit intersects closely with technology leadership: enterprise financial systems (ERP, FP&A platforms, treasury management systems) sit at the boundary of IT and Finance governance; cloud and technology spend increasingly falls under FinOps disciplines that the CFO co-owns with the CIO and CTO; and major technology investment decisions require the CFO's sign-off through capital allocation and business case governance processes.

## Role Scope & Boundaries

- **Scope of Influence:** Organisation-wide — full financial strategy, capital structure, and reporting authority
- **Experience Anchor:** 15+ years of progressive finance leadership, typically 5+ years at CFO or deputy-CFO level — operates independently, accountable to the CEO and Board Audit Committee
- **Out of Scope:** Corporate strategy direction and business model decisions (CEO and Board-owned); operational strategy and business unit performance improvement (COO and business leaders-owned); technology product strategy and engineering investment prioritisation (CTO-owned)
- **Escalates To:** CEO and Board Audit/Risk Committee — material financial risk, capital structure decisions, and statutory reporting matters
- **Escalated To By:** CIO, CTO, SVP of Technology, and business unit leaders on investment business cases and budget requests requiring capital allocation approval

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures the organisation's financial strategy, capital allocation, reporting, and risk management are sound, compliant, and directly enabling the business to achieve its strategic objectives — while protecting shareholder and stakeholder value
- **Value Metrics:** Revenue, EBITDA, free cash flow, return on invested capital (ROIC), working capital efficiency, cost-to-income ratio, financial audit outcome, regulatory financial compliance posture, and accuracy of financial forecasting
- **Key Stakeholders:** CEO, Board of Directors and Audit/Risk Committee, CIO (IT cost governance and ERP financial systems), CTO (technology investment and FinOps), COO (operational cost management and financial controls), CISO (financial risk and regulatory compliance), external auditors, banking and lending partners, investors, and regulatory bodies
- **Processes Supported:** Financial planning and analysis (FP&A), management accounting and reporting, statutory and regulatory financial reporting, treasury and liquidity management, capital allocation and investment governance, tax strategy and compliance, internal financial controls and audit, enterprise risk management (financial dimensions), and M&A financial due diligence

## Key Responsibilities

- Own and evolve the organisation's financial strategy — including multi-year financial planning, capital structure optimisation, funding and liquidity strategy, and financial targets — aligned to the overall business strategy and Board-approved risk appetite
- Hold the organisation's financial reporting obligations — statutory accounts, management reporting, investor and regulatory disclosures — ensuring accuracy, timeliness, and full compliance with applicable accounting standards (IFRS, GAAP) and regulatory requirements
- Own the annual budgeting and quarterly forecasting cycle across the enterprise, partnering with all functional leaders to translate operational plans into financial commitments and holding the organisation to financial discipline
- Govern capital allocation — including major investment approvals, technology and infrastructure capex, M&A activity, and return-on-investment measurement — ensuring all significant financial commitments are subject to rigorous business case evaluation and post-investment review
- Manage treasury, cash flow, and liquidity — including banking relationships, debt facilities, FX risk, and working capital optimisation — to ensure the organisation maintains financial resilience and operational flexibility
- Own the enterprise's internal financial controls framework and maintain an effective internal audit function, ensuring the control environment meets the requirements of external auditors and relevant regulators
- Partner with the CIO on ERP financial systems strategy and enterprise IT investment governance; partner with the CTO on technology capital investment, build-vs-buy financial evaluation, and FinOps cloud cost governance
- Lead investor relations and external financial communication for publicly listed organisations, or financial reporting to shareholders, banking partners, and Board for private organisations
- Report to the CEO and Board (Audit Committee, Risk Committee) on financial performance, financial risk, capital adequacy, and compliance — providing transparent, decision-ready financial insight at all times
- Build and develop the finance leadership team — including FP&A, Group Reporting, Treasury, Tax, and Internal Audit functions — maintaining a culture of financial integrity, commercial rigor, and continuous improvement

## Required Skills & Qualifications

**Technical Skills:**

- Deep expertise in financial accounting (IFRS and/or GAAP), financial reporting standards, and statutory compliance obligations relevant to the organisation's sector and jurisdiction(s)
- Strong command of financial planning and analysis — including long-range planning, scenario modelling, driver-based forecasting, and financial performance management at enterprise scale
- Treasury and capital markets knowledge — including cash flow management, debt instruments, FX hedging, and banking relationship management — sufficient to own the organisation's liquidity and capital structure strategy
- Understanding of enterprise financial systems — ERP platforms (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics), FP&A tools (Anaplan, Adaptive Insights), and treasury management systems — at a level sufficient to govern their strategic direction and ensure financial data integrity
- Familiarity with FinOps principles and cloud cost governance in partnership with CIO/CTO, particularly as cloud and technology spend grows as a proportion of total operating cost
- Knowledge of M&A financial due diligence, deal structuring, and post-merger integration financial governance

**Soft Skills:**

- Executive leadership — the ability to lead a high-performing finance function, set high standards of financial integrity and commercial rigor, and hold the organisation accountable to its financial commitments
- Strategic influence — able to translate complex financial analysis into clear business insight that shapes executive and Board decision-making, without defaulting to finance jargon
- Commercial acumen and business partnering — deeply engaged with operational realities, able to act as a genuine strategic partner to business leaders rather than a financial gatekeeper
- Resilience under pressure — able to lead the finance function confidently through external audit cycles, regulatory scrutiny, M&A activity, or financial distress situations
- Integrity and independence — maintaining the highest standards of financial reporting accuracy and transparency, even under commercial or stakeholder pressure

### Qualifications

**Education:**

Degree in Finance, Accounting, Economics, or a related field. A postgraduate qualification (MBA, MSc in Finance, or equivalent) is advantageous. Chartered accountancy qualification (ACA, ACCA, CIMA, CPA, or equivalent) is typically expected at this level.

**Certifications:**

| Certification | Body | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| ACA (Associate Chartered Accountant) | ICAEW | Financial reporting and audit |
| ACCA (Association of Chartered Certified Accountants) | ACCA Global | Financial reporting and compliance |
| CIMA (Chartered Institute of Management Accountants) | CIMA | Management accounting and business finance |
| CPA (Certified Public Accountant) | AICPA | US-based financial reporting and audit |
| CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst) | CFA Institute | Investment analysis and capital markets |
| FinOps Certified Practitioner | FinOps Foundation | Cloud cost governance |

**Experience:**

15+ years of progressive finance leadership experience, with at least 5 years in a senior finance executive role (CFO, VP of Finance, Group Financial Controller, or equivalent). Proven track record of owning enterprise financial reporting, managing external audit and regulatory relationships, governing capital allocation at scale, and partnering with CEOs and Boards through complex financial cycles including growth phases, restructuring, or M&A activity.

## Key Performance Indicators

| Metric | Target | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Financial forecast accuracy | Variance between forecast and actuals within ±3% at the revenue and EBITDA line | Quarterly |
| On-time statutory reporting | All statutory filings submitted on or before regulatory deadlines | Annual |
| External audit outcome | Clean audit opinion; no material misstatements or significant control deficiencies | Annual |
| Free cash flow | At or above Board-approved targets; positive trend year-on-year | Quarterly |
| Cost-to-income ratio | At or below industry benchmark; year-on-year improvement trend | Annual |
| Internal audit findings remediation | ≥90% of agreed audit actions completed within target date | Quarterly |
| Budget cycle completion | Annual budget approved by Board within agreed governance timeline | Annual |
| Regulatory financial compliance | Zero material compliance breaches; all regulatory returns filed on time | Annual |

## Career Development Path

**Previous Roles:**

- Group Financial Controller or VP of Finance with enterprise-wide scope and direct Board engagement
- Finance Director or Head of FP&A with a strong track record of financial planning, reporting, and business partnering at scale
- CFO of a smaller or less complex organisation, stepping up in scale, sector complexity, or regulatory burden
- Investment banker, private equity finance lead, or M&A specialist moving into an operational CFO role
- Big Four audit partner or senior manager with a client portfolio in the relevant sector, transitioning to industry

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Group CFO in a larger enterprise or group holding company with multiple business units and geographies
- CEO in an organisation where the CFO has built strong enterprise-wide strategic influence and P&L accountability
- Non-executive Director or Audit Committee Chair with a financial governance and risk specialism
- Private equity Operating Partner or Portfolio CFO with broad investment and value creation mandate
- Finance advisory or consulting partner role focused on enterprise financial transformation and M&A

## Typical Day-to-Day Activities

- Reviewing financial performance, cash position, and forecast variance with FP&A and treasury teams
- Chairing capital allocation and investment governance forums, including technology business-case reviews
- Preparing Board, audit committee, and investor reporting: results, guidance, and financial risk posture
- Working with the CEO on enterprise strategy, operating plan trade-offs, and M&A financial modelling
- Engaging auditors, banks, rating agencies, and institutional investors
- Reviewing enterprise cost programmes, including cloud and technology spend governance with the CTO/CIO
- Overseeing financial controls, compliance, and tax posture with controllers and internal audit
- Holding 1:1s with finance leadership and driving finance function transformation and automation

## Remote Work Considerations

- **Remote Eligibility:** Hybrid; significant on-site presence required for Board and audit committee cycles, results announcements, executive leadership sessions, and investor engagements
- **Collaboration Tools:** Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, ERP and planning platforms, and executive dashboards for financial reporting and governance
- **On-Site Requirements:** Regular presence at headquarters for Board, audit committee, and executive team meetings; travel for investor roadshows, banking relationships, and M&A activity
- **Time Zone Flexibility:** Standard business hours with extended availability during close, results, audit, and M&A periods
- **On-Call / Operational Demands:** Escalation point for material financial events, liquidity issues, and financially significant incidents, including cyber events with financial-disclosure implications

## Common Challenges

- Balancing the tension between maintaining rigorous financial controls and compliance standards while also enabling the business to move at pace on strategic investment decisions and commercial opportunities
- Managing the growing complexity and cost of enterprise financial systems — particularly ERP modernisation, FP&A tooling evolution, and the integration of financial data across a fragmented application landscape — in partnership with the CIO
- Exercising meaningful governance over technology and cloud spend as it becomes a material proportion of the operating cost base, often requiring new FinOps disciplines and closer partnership with the CTO and CIO than traditional CFO operating models anticipated
- Maintaining forecast accuracy and financial credibility with the Board in volatile macroeconomic conditions, where revenue visibility is limited and cost pressures are unpredictable
- Navigating the CFO's expanding mandate — beyond traditional accounting and reporting — into areas such as ESG financial reporting, digital finance transformation, and enterprise data governance, which require new capabilities alongside the traditional finance skill set

## Example Projects

- Leading an enterprise ERP financial systems modernisation — replacing a legacy general ledger and reporting platform with a modern cloud ERP (SAP S/4HANA Finance, Oracle Cloud Financials, or Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance), including financial data migration, internal controls redesign, and the elimination of manual reporting workarounds
- Designing and implementing a group-wide FP&A transformation — deploying a driver-based planning platform (Anaplan or Adaptive Insights), connecting it to operational data sources, and dramatically reducing the budget cycle timeline and forecast error rate
- Establishing a FinOps governance framework in partnership with the CTO and CIO — creating financial visibility into cloud consumption, implementing chargeback or showback models for cloud cost allocation, and delivering a measurable reduction in cloud unit economics
- Executing a major M&A transaction from financial due diligence through to post-merger financial integration — including accounting policy alignment, consolidated reporting design, intercompany elimination, and finance team integration
- Building and embedding an enterprise internal controls framework — implementing a controls-over-financial-reporting (COFR) programme aligned to SOX or equivalent standards, achieving a clean internal audit opinion, and materially reducing the risk of financial misstatement
