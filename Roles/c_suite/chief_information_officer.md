## Role Overview

The Chief Information Officer (CIO) is the senior executive accountable for the organisation's IT operations, enterprise systems, IT infrastructure, and internal IT service delivery. The CIO owns the strategic direction and operational performance of IT — including enterprise platforms such as ERP, HRIS, ITSM, and collaboration tooling — and ensures the internal IT function is reliable, cost-effective, secure, and fully aligned to the operational needs of the business. The CIO typically takes an internally-facing perspective, focused on operational IT, employee digital experience, IT governance, service management maturity, and the IT vendor relationships that underpin the organisation's day-to-day operations.

In most organisations the CIO reports directly to the CEO and operates as a peer of the CTO. Where both roles exist, the CTO is typically accountable for technology product strategy and engineering capability, while the CIO is accountable for enterprise IT operations, internal systems, and IT service delivery. In some organisations — particularly those where IT is viewed primarily as an operational cost centre — the CIO may report to the COO rather than the CEO; both models are valid. In organisations where only one senior technology executive exists (for example, an SVP of Technology), that role typically combines the mandates of both CIO and CTO; this is explicitly noted in the SVP of Technology role definition.

In some governance models the CISO reports to the CIO, particularly where security is positioned as an IT operational risk and compliance function rather than an engineering or product concern.

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures the organisation's internal IT infrastructure, enterprise systems, and IT services are reliable, secure, cost-effective, and aligned to business operations — enabling the workforce to operate effectively, regulatory obligations to be met, and the business to scale without IT becoming a constraint
- **Value Metrics:** IT service availability, cost per user, digital employee experience score, IT project delivery performance, Mean Time to Resolve (MTTR) for IT incidents, regulatory IT compliance posture, and IT vendor performance against SLAs
- **Key Stakeholders:** CEO, CFO (IT cost governance and financial systems), COO (operational continuity), CPO (HRIS and employee experience), CTO (architecture alignment and shared infrastructure), CISO, business operations leaders, end users across the organisation, and key enterprise system vendors and system integrators
- **Processes Supported:** IT service management (ITIL-based), enterprise system lifecycle management (ERP, HRIS, ITSM, collaboration), IT budget planning and vendor governance, IT governance and audit, business continuity and disaster recovery planning, IT project portfolio management, and digital workplace strategy

## Key Responsibilities

- Own and evolve the enterprise IT strategy — covering IT infrastructure, enterprise systems, IT operations, and internal digital services — aligned to the operational needs of the business and multi-year business strategy
- Hold the IT budget, including operating expenditure for infrastructure, enterprise systems licensing, managed services, and IT headcount, as well as capital expenditure for major system implementations and infrastructure refresh
- Ensure IT service availability, reliability, and performance — owning SLAs for all internal IT services and maintaining operational resilience through business continuity planning and disaster recovery capability
- Own the lifecycle management of enterprise systems — including ERP, HRIS, ITSM, and collaboration platforms — covering vendor strategy, upgrade and migration planning, and system integration governance
- Deliver and continuously improve the digital employee experience — end-user computing, productivity tooling, service desk quality, and IT onboarding — as a key enabler of workforce productivity and employee satisfaction
- Own IT governance frameworks, including ITIL-based service management practices, COBIT-based IT governance, and compliance with IT regulatory requirements relevant to the organisation's sector
- Manage the IT vendor and system integrator portfolio — negotiating enterprise agreements, managing performance, and ensuring value for money across all major IT investments
- Report to the CEO (and, where relevant, the CFO, COO, and Board Audit Committee) on IT risk, IT investment performance, service availability, and regulatory compliance posture
- Build and develop the IT leadership team — including IT operations leads, enterprise application managers, and service management professionals — creating a culture of operational excellence and continuous improvement
- Collaborate with the CTO (where both roles exist) on shared infrastructure strategy, cloud governance, and enterprise architecture alignment to ensure technology product and IT operations work cohesively

## Key Decisions and Accountabilities

> Clarifies what this role **owns** (decides independently) vs. **advises on** (input without final authority).

| Owns | Advises On |
|---|---|
| Enterprise IT strategy: infrastructure, enterprise systems, IT operations, and internal digital services | Technology product strategy and external-facing platform direction (CTO-owned) |
| IT budget: infrastructure, enterprise systems, managed services, and IT headcount | Enterprise-wide capital allocation and financial governance (CFO and CEO-owned) |
| IT service management framework, SLAs, and operational IT resilience standards | Business process design and operational change management (COO and business leaders-owned) |
| Enterprise system lifecycle decisions: ERP, HRIS, ITSM, and collaboration platform strategy | Enterprise architecture direction and technology standards (CTO/TAL-owned, or jointly owned where CIO/CTO boundary exists) |
| IT vendor portfolio management and enterprise system integrator relationships | Strategic technology partnerships beyond operational IT (CTO-owned) |
| IT governance framework (ITIL, COBIT) and regulatory IT compliance posture | Enterprise risk appetite and board-level risk framework (CEO and CRO-owned) |
| IT leadership team structure, hiring, and development | Broader technology talent strategy and engineering culture (CTO-owned) |

## Required Skills

**Technical Skills:**

- Strong understanding of enterprise IT architecture, including on-premise and cloud infrastructure, enterprise systems integration, and IT operations management at scale
- Deep familiarity with IT service management frameworks (ITIL 4) and IT governance frameworks (COBIT) — sufficient to own and mature the organisation's IT operations practices and governance posture
- Experience managing enterprise system lifecycles — ERP (SAP, Oracle), HRIS (Workday, SuccessFactors), ITSM (ServiceNow), and collaboration platforms (Microsoft 365) — at strategic leadership level, including vendor selection, implementation oversight, and long-term roadmap ownership
- Understanding of cloud operations, hybrid infrastructure, and cloud cost governance (FinOps) in an IT operational context — including cloud migration strategy for enterprise systems
- Familiarity with IT regulatory and compliance obligations relevant to the organisation's sector, including GDPR (data residency and processing), DORA (digital operational resilience), and relevant audit standards (ISO 27001, SOC 2)

**Soft Skills:**

- Operational leadership — the ability to drive service excellence, continuous improvement, and cost efficiency across complex IT operations at scale, and to hold IT teams to high standards of performance and accountability
- Executive communication — able to translate IT operational performance, risk, and investment into business and Board-appropriate language for non-technical stakeholders
- Strong commercial acumen — skilled at vendor negotiation, total cost of ownership analysis, and IT investment governance to maximise value from IT spend
- Collaborative partnership with business operations leaders — deeply aligned to the operational needs of the organisation's functions (Finance, HR, Operations, Legal) and able to position IT as an enabler rather than a constraint
- Resilience and decisiveness during IT incidents, major system outages, or IT-related compliance situations — including the ability to lead effective executive escalation and business communication

## Qualifications

**Education:**

Degree in Information Technology, Computer Science, Business Information Systems, or a related field. A postgraduate qualification (MBA, MSc in IT Management or equivalent) is advantageous and strengthens the commercial and governance credibility required at this level.

**Certifications:**

| Certification | Body | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| ITIL 4 Managing Professional or Strategic Leader | Axelos / PeopleCert | IT service management leadership |
| COBIT 2019 Design and Implementation | ISACA | IT governance framework |
| CGEIT (Certified in Governance of Enterprise IT) | ISACA | Enterprise IT governance |
| FinOps Certified Practitioner | FinOps Foundation | Cloud cost governance |
| PMP or MSP Practitioner | PMI / Axelos | IT project and programme management |
| Microsoft 365 Certified: Enterprise Administrator | Microsoft | Enterprise collaboration and productivity platforms |

**Experience:**

15+ years of progressive IT leadership experience, with at least 5 years in a senior IT executive role (CIO, VP of IT, Head of IT, or equivalent). Proven track record of owning enterprise IT operations, managing large IT vendor and system integrator relationships, and delivering IT service improvement and enterprise system transformation programmes in complex, multi-site organisations.

## KPIs and Success Metrics

| Metric | Target | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| IT service availability | ≥99.9% for critical enterprise systems and infrastructure | Monthly |
| Mean Time to Resolve (MTTR) — P1 IT incidents | P1 incidents resolved within agreed SLA (e.g., <4 hours) | Monthly |
| Cost per user (IT cost efficiency) | At or below industry benchmark; year-on-year improvement trend | Annual |
| Digital employee experience score | Positive trend; at or above internal satisfaction target | Annual |
| IT project delivery performance | ≥80% of IT projects delivered on time and within budget | Quarterly |
| Regulatory IT compliance | Zero material compliance breaches; audit findings remediated within agreed SLA | Annual |
| IT vendor performance | ≥90% of key vendors meeting contracted SLA targets | Quarterly |
| Business continuity / DR test results | All critical systems recovered within agreed RTO/RPO in annual DR test | Annual |

## Career Progression

**Previous Roles:**

- VP of IT or Head of IT Operations with enterprise-wide scope and direct stakeholder engagement at C-suite level
- IT Director or Senior IT Manager with a proven track record of service improvement, enterprise system delivery, and IT governance maturity
- Enterprise Architect or Solutions Architect with a strong operational IT background stepping up to IT leadership scope
- CIO in a smaller or less complex organisation, stepping up in scale, sector complexity, or regulatory burden
- Programme Director or IT PMO Lead with P&L experience, vendor management, and delivery governance track record

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Group CIO in a larger enterprise or group holding company with multiple business units and geographies
- COO in an IT-intensive organisation where technology and operational leadership are converging
- CEO of a managed services provider, technology services business, or IT-led professional services firm
- Non-executive Director or Board Advisor with a technology governance and IT risk specialism
- Technology advisory or management consulting partner role focused on enterprise IT strategy and transformation

## Common Challenges

- Managing the tension between delivering operational IT stability and cost efficiency while the business demands increasing pace of enterprise system change, digital workplace investment, and IT-enabled process transformation
- Rationalising a complex legacy enterprise system estate — including end-of-life ERP versions, redundant operational systems, and accumulated technical debt in IT infrastructure — in a resource and budget-constrained environment
- Demonstrating the business value of IT investment when IT outcomes are typically measured by the absence of failure (availability, reliability, compliance) rather than positive revenue or growth contribution
- Navigating the CTO/CIO boundary in organisations where both roles exist — particularly on cloud infrastructure ownership, enterprise data platforms, shared architecture decisions, and vendor relationships that span both domains
- Maintaining IT vendor and system integrator relationships as commercially healthy partnerships while also enforcing rigorous SLA performance management and value-for-money governance

## Example Projects

- Leading an enterprise ERP modernisation programme — migrating from a legacy on-premise ERP to a modern cloud-based platform (e.g., SAP S/4HANA or Oracle Cloud ERP), including vendor selection, business process redesign oversight, IT delivery governance, and change management
- Designing and implementing an IT service management transformation — adopting ITIL 4 practices across the IT function, implementing a new ITSM platform (ServiceNow), and achieving measurable improvements in MTTR, first-contact resolution, and internal customer satisfaction scores
- Delivering a digital workplace programme — rolling out Microsoft 365 across the enterprise, modernising end-user computing, decommissioning legacy collaboration tools, and measurably improving the digital employee experience score
- Building and testing an enterprise-wide IT business continuity and disaster recovery capability — including cloud-based DR infrastructure, runbook documentation, and successful annual failover testing for all Tier 1 critical systems
- Conducting an IT vendor portfolio rationalisation — systematically reviewing all enterprise IT contracts, consolidating redundant vendors, renegotiating strategic agreements at enterprise scale, and delivering a measurable reduction in total IT cost per user
