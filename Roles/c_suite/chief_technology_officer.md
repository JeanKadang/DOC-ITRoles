## Role Overview

The Chief Technology Officer (CTO) is the senior executive accountable for the organisation's technology product strategy, engineering capability, innovation agenda, and enterprise architecture governance. The CTO owns the technology vision for the business — setting direction for product platforms, engineering culture, R&D investment, and technical standards — and typically takes an externally-facing perspective, focused on customer-facing technology, competitive differentiation through engineering capability, and the strategic technology partnerships that drive innovation.

In most organisations the CTO reports directly to the CEO and operates as a peer of the CIO (where both roles exist). The CTO is primarily accountable for _how_ the organisation uses technology to build products and create competitive advantage, while the CIO is accountable for the reliability, cost, and governance of internal IT systems and operations. In organisations where only one senior technology executive exists — such as an SVP of Technology — that role typically combines the mandates of both CTO and CIO; this is explicitly noted in the SVP of Technology role definition.

In some governance models the CISO reports to the CTO, particularly where security is positioned as a product and engineering concern (DevSecOps, security architecture, and platform security) rather than primarily an operational compliance function.

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures the organisation maintains and advances its technical capability, product platform competitiveness, and engineering culture — translating business strategy into technology direction and enabling the organisation to innovate, scale, and differentiate through technology
- **Value Metrics:** Platform reliability and availability, engineering velocity and deployment frequency, innovation pipeline health, technical debt reduction trend, architecture compliance rate, R&D investment return, and technology talent attraction and retention
- **Key Stakeholders:** CEO, CIO (where both roles exist), CPO (Chief Product Officer), CISO, business unit heads, Product Area Leads, Technical Area Lead, principal engineers, strategic technology vendors, cloud providers, and technology partners
- **Processes Supported:** Technology strategy and product roadmap governance, enterprise architecture review and governance, engineering capability and talent planning, R&D and innovation programme management, technology standards definition, strategic technology vendor and partner management, and technical due diligence for M&A

## Key Responsibilities

- Own and evolve the enterprise technology product vision and multi-year technology roadmap, ensuring alignment with business strategy and market direction
- Set the engineering culture, standards, and ways of working — including engineering principles, development practices, and the organisation's approach to technical excellence and developer experience
- Own enterprise architecture governance, including technology standards, platform decisions, and architecture review processes across all engineering and product domains
- Lead the R&D and innovation agenda — identifying emerging technologies (AI, ML, cloud-native, data platforms) with strategic relevance and building investment cases for adoption
- Hold the technology engineering budget, including capital and operating expenditure for engineering headcount, tooling, platform infrastructure, and R&D
- Manage executive relationships with strategic technology vendors, cloud providers, and technology partners — negotiating strategic agreements and shaping the organisation's technology partnership strategy
- Act as the external technology spokesperson for the organisation, representing technical capability and innovation to customers, investors, analysts, and the media
- Work with the CEO on technology-related M&A and investment opportunities, including providing technical due diligence leadership and integration strategy for acquired technology assets
- Report to the CEO on technology product performance, engineering health, innovation pipeline, and technology risk at an executive level
- Build and develop the senior engineering leadership team — including Product Area Leads, Technical Area Lead, and principal engineers — creating succession depth and a high-performance engineering culture

## Key Decisions and Accountabilities

| Owns | Advises On |
|---|---|

| Engineering budget: headcount, tooling, platform infrastructure, and R&D investment | Enterprise-wide capital allocation and financial governance (CFO and CEO-owned) |
| Enterprise architecture governance: technology standards, platform selection, and architecture review processes | Architecture domain detail and workload-level technical decisions (Enterprise Architect and TAL-owned) |

| Senior engineering leadership hiring, structure, and succession planning | Broader executive appointments and HR policy (CEO and CPO-owned) |
| External representation as technology spokesperson — investors, analysts, media, and strategic events | Corporate communications strategy and brand messaging (CMO-owned) |

## Required Skills

**Technical Skills:**

- Deep understanding of modern software engineering practices, cloud-native architecture, and enterprise platform design — sufficient to set credible technical direction and make informed architecture investment decisions
- Strong appreciation of AI/ML, data platform strategy, and developer experience — including how these capabilities create competitive differentiation and long-term platform advantage
- Familiarity with cloud platform strategy across major hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP), including FinOps principles, cloud governance models, and platform engineering practices
- Understanding of security architecture principles, DevSecOps, and the relationship between engineering practices and security posture — sufficient to engage credibly with the CISO on product security requirements
- Experience with enterprise architecture governance, including technology standards definition, reference architectures, architecture decision records, and technology lifecycle management

**Soft Skills:**

- Visionary technical leadership — the ability to set an inspiring and credible engineering direction and mobilise large engineering organisations around a shared technical vision
- Executive communication — fluent at translating complex technical topics into strategic business language for the CEO, Board, investors, and media audiences
- Commercial acumen — understanding the relationship between technology investment, platform competitiveness, and business revenue, margin, and market position
- Talent leadership — deep commitment to engineering talent development, culture, and the creation of high-performing, diverse engineering communities
- Strategic influencing — able to shape corporate strategy from a technology perspective and make compelling cases for engineering investment in competitive resource allocation discussions

## Qualifications

**Education:**

Degree in Computer Science, Software Engineering, Mathematics, or a related technical field. A postgraduate qualification (MBA, MSc) is advantageous but not required where equivalent depth of practical experience and executive track record exists.

**Certifications:**

| Certification | Body | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| TOGAF Foundation or Practitioner | The Open Group | Enterprise architecture governance |
| AWS / Azure / GCP Professional Architect | AWS / Microsoft / Google | Cloud platform strategy credibility |
| SAFe Program Consultant (SPC) or LPM | Scaled Agile Inc. | Engineering at scale and Lean Portfolio Management |
| MIT Sloan CTO Executive Programme | MIT Sloan | Executive leadership and business strategy for technology leaders |
| Board-level technology governance programmes (IoD, NACD) | IoD / NACD | Governance credibility at CEO and Board level |

**Experience:**

15+ years of progressive technology and engineering leadership experience, with at least 5 years in a senior technology executive role (VP of Engineering, CTO, or equivalent). Proven track record of owning enterprise technology strategy, leading large engineering organisations, and delivering technology-led business outcomes at scale.

## Career Progression

**Previous Roles:**

- VP of Engineering or Head of Engineering with enterprise-wide technical scope and executive stakeholder engagement
- Technical Area Lead (TAL) or Principal/Chief Architect with a track record of enterprise architecture strategy and stepping up to executive scope
- CTO in a smaller or less complex organisation, stepping up in scale or sector complexity
- Engineering Director or Senior Engineering Manager with a demonstrable track record of technical strategy delivery and team leadership
- Product Area Lead with a strong technical background and a clear engineering leadership development track record

**Potential Next Roles:**

- Group CTO in a larger enterprise or group holding company with multiple technology domains
- Chief Executive Officer in a technology-led or engineering-led organisation
- Non-executive Director or Board Advisor with a technology and engineering specialism
- Technology-focused venture capital partner or private equity operating partner
- Technology entrepreneur, founder, or executive-level investor

## Interactions with Other Roles

| Role | Nature of Interaction |
|---|---|
| CEO | Primary reporting line; technology product strategy, innovation pipeline, and engineering risk |
| CIO | Close peer collaboration on enterprise architecture, cloud strategy, and shared infrastructure; joint decisions where product engineering and IT operations overlap |
| CISO | Security architecture standards, DevSecOps requirements, and security posture of product platforms; CISO may report to CTO in some governance models |
| CPO (Chief Product Officer) | Joint ownership of product platform direction; CTO owns technical strategy and engineering execution, CPO owns product vision and customer outcomes |
| Product Area Leads (PALs) | Direct or indirect leadership of engineering delivery; PALs operate within the engineering standards and architecture direction set by the CTO |
| Technical Area Lead (TAL) | Close partnership on enterprise architecture, engineering standards, principal engineering community, and technology governance |
| Strategic technology vendors / cloud providers | Executive relationship management for platform strategy, roadmap alignment, and strategic commercial agreements |
| Board / Investors | Technical credibility, innovation narrative, and technology risk assurance for investor and Board engagement |

## Common Challenges

- Balancing near-term engineering delivery demands against long-term platform investment and technical debt reduction — particularly when business stakeholders prioritise feature velocity over architecture health
- Maintaining engineering culture, talent, and technical rigour at scale while the organisation grows through hiring, acquisition, or rapid strategic change
- Driving architecture coherence and technology standards adoption across multiple autonomous product area teams without becoming a bottleneck or creating a compliance overhead
- Making credible investment cases for R&D and innovation in an environment where ROI timelines are long, outcomes are uncertain, and competing business priorities are immediate
- Managing the boundary with the CIO role — particularly on shared cloud infrastructure, enterprise data platforms, and cross-functional architecture decisions — in organisations where both CTO and CIO exist

## Example Projects

- Leading the design and adoption of a new cloud-native platform strategy — including architecture standards definition, target state documentation, and a migration roadmap for legacy platforms
- Sponsoring an enterprise AI/ML capability programme — defining the organisation's approach to AI adoption, building ML platform infrastructure, establishing responsible AI engineering standards, and delivering the first production AI-powered products
- Providing technical due diligence leadership on an M&A target — evaluating technology stack quality, engineering team capability, platform scalability, security posture, and integration complexity
- Building and launching an engineering excellence programme — including developer experience tooling, CI/CD platform engineering, internal developer platform (IDP) adoption, and engineering community practices
- Establishing an enterprise architecture review board and technology standards framework to govern platform decisions, technology selection, and architecture compliance across all product areas and engineering teams
