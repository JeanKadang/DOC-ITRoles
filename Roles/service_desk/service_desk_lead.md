## Role Overview

The Service Desk Lead owns the service desk as an operational function: staffing and shift coverage, ticket-handling SLAs, the Tier-1/Tier-2/Tier-3 escalation model, and the desk's standing as the single front door for every employee's technology problem. This role is named directly across the End User & Workplace chapter — Client Platform, Endpoint Management, and Modern Workplace roles all cite the Service Desk Lead as the owner of escalation boundaries and support-model decisions that affect their own Tier-2/3 workload. The Service Desk Lead does not own the engineering behind any of those domains; the role owns the operational front line that determines how quickly and accurately problems reach the people who do.

## Role Scope & Boundaries

- **Scope of Influence:** Domain — owns the service desk's operating model, staffing, and SLAs; does not own the technical standards of the domains it escalates into (Client Platform, Endpoint Management, Security, Identity).
- **Experience Anchor:** 4+ years in IT service desk or support operations, including at least 1–2 years in a team-lead or shift-supervisor capacity; operates independently setting the desk's operating model within chapter-level guardrails.
- **Out of Scope:** Does not own OS, endpoint, identity, or security engineering standards (owned by the respective domain architects); does not own the ITSM platform's technical architecture (owned by Service Management / ITSM Configuration, though this role owns how the desk uses it day-to-day); does not declare major incidents (owned by the Major Incident Manager, though this role owns the desk's detection-and-escalation path into that process).
- **Escalates To:** End User & Workplace Chapter Lead (for staffing budget, cross-domain escalation disputes, or SLA target changes requiring chapter-level sign-off).
- **Escalated To By:** Service Desk Senior Analysts (for recurring issues needing a process or standard-change fix, not just a ticket fix, and for staffing/coverage gaps).
- **People-management exception:** across the rest of the catalogue, Senior Engineers provide day-to-day technical guidance while formal line management sits with the Chapter Lead. The Service Desk Lead is the deliberate exception and holds line management directly — hiring, onboarding and career progression for Analysts and Senior Analysts. The desk runs a shift rota with its own progression ladder and a headcount model driven by ticket volume rather than project demand, none of which a Chapter Lead one level removed can staff in real time. Confirmed as intentional rather than drift when the catalogue's management model was reviewed (#148).

## Business Impact

- **Business Objective:** Ensures every employee has a fast, consistent, and correctly-routed path to technology help — the desk is the organization's single measurable front door for IT support, and its performance shapes how the entire IT function is perceived.
- **Value Metrics:** Overall desk SLA attainment (response and resolution), CSAT trend across the desk, ticket volume per FTE, Tier-1 first-contact-resolution rate, correctly-scoped Tier-2/3 escalation rate, staff retention and time-to-productivity for new Analysts.
- **Key Stakeholders:** End User & Workplace Chapter Lead, Client Platform Architect, Endpoint Management Architect, Major Incident Manager, Change / Release Manager, Vendor / Supplier / IT Asset Manager, HR/People function, every employee as an end user.
- **Processes Supported:** Service desk staffing and rota management, SLA and KPI governance for Tier-1/2 support, Tier-2/3 escalation boundary definition, knowledge base and self-service portal governance, standard-change catalogue maintenance for desk-initiated changes, new-starter/leaver ticket process ownership.

## Key Responsibilities

- Own the service desk staffing model: headcount, shift rota, and coverage hours, ensuring response times hold across peak periods (Monday mornings, new-starter cohorts, major software rollouts).
- Define and maintain the Tier-1/Tier-2/Tier-3 escalation boundaries in partnership with the Client Platform Architect and Endpoint Management Architect, so escalations land with the right team the first time.
- Set and govern the desk's SLA and KPI framework: response times, resolution targets, CSAT thresholds, and first-contact-resolution goals — reporting attainment to the Chapter Lead.
- Run the desk's people management: hiring, onboarding, and career progression for Analysts and Senior Analysts, including the Analyst → Senior Analyst progression path.
- Own the knowledge base and self-service portal's structure and governance, ensuring content stays current and self-service deflection keeps growing.
- Partner with the Change / Release Manager to maintain the standard change catalogue for desk-initiated changes (standard software installs, routine access requests) that don't need full change approval.
- Own the desk's role in major-incident detection: ensure Analysts and Senior Analysts know the escalation trigger for the Major Incident Manager, and that the desk can staff up communication/triage support during a live major incident.
- Coordinate with the Vendor / Supplier / IT Asset Manager on hardware/entitlement checks that gate common ticket types (loaner equipment, software license availability).
- Report desk performance, staffing risk, and recurring systemic issues to the End User & Workplace Chapter Lead as input to the chapter's roadmap and investment decisions.

## Key Decisions & Accountabilities

> Clarifies what this role **owns** (decides independently) vs. **advises on** (input without final authority).

| Owns | Advises On |
|---|---|
| Service desk support model, staffing levels, and shift/rota design | OS-level and endpoint engineering standards that shape what Tier-2/3 receives (owned by Client Platform Architect / Endpoint Management Architect) |
| Tier-1/Tier-2/Tier-3 escalation boundary definition, agreed jointly with Client Platform Architect | ITSM platform technical architecture and tooling selection (owned by Service Management / ITSM Configuration) |
| Desk SLA/KPI framework, targets, and attainment reporting | Major incident declaration criteria and incident bridge management (owned by Major Incident Manager) |
| Knowledge base and self-service portal governance and structure | Standard change catalogue approval criteria (owned jointly with Change / Release Manager) |
| Analyst/Senior Analyst hiring, staffing budget requests, and career progression within the desk | Chapter-level headcount budget and cross-chapter escalation policy (owned by End User & Workplace Chapter Lead) |

## Required Skills & Qualifications

**Technical Skills:**

- Strong operational understanding of ITSM platform configuration (ServiceNow or equivalent): SLA workflows, categorization schemas, knowledge base structure, reporting dashboards
- Working knowledge of the technical domains the desk escalates into — Windows/macOS end-user environments, Microsoft 365, endpoint management, identity basics — sufficient to set accurate escalation criteria
- Experience with workforce/shift scheduling for a 24/5 or 24/7 support operation
- Familiarity with ITIL 4 practices, particularly incident management, request fulfilment, and continual improvement
- Data literacy sufficient to build and interpret SLA/KPI dashboards and identify trends requiring intervention

**Soft Skills and Leadership:**

- People management: hiring, coaching, performance management, and career development for a front-line technical team.
- Calm, decisive escalation judgment — knowing when a pattern is a genuine incident versus noise, and communicating that decision confidently under pressure.
- Cross-team negotiation skill — the escalation boundary agreements with Client Platform and Endpoint Management require balancing desk workload against receiving teams' capacity.
- Clear reporting and presentation skill for chapter-level and executive-visible SLA/CSAT reporting.

**Technology Proficiency Levels:**

**Expert level required:**

- ITSM ticketing platform administration and SLA/reporting configuration (ServiceNow or equivalent)

**Proficient level required:**

- Workforce/shift scheduling tooling
- Windows/macOS and Microsoft 365 end-user support concepts

**Working Knowledge required:**

- Endpoint management (Intune) and identity (Entra ID) concepts sufficient to set escalation criteria

**Awareness level expected:**

- ITIL 4 major incident and problem management practices
- standard-change governance models

## Interactions with Other Roles

> **Interaction Mode** describes the direction/nature of the relationship: **Collaborates** (peer-to-peer), **Consumes From** (relies on the other role's output/service), **Provides To** (delivers a service the other role consumes), **Governed By** (subject to the other role's standards/approval), or **Escalates To** (routes unresolved issues upward).

| Role | Nature of Interaction | Interaction Mode |
|---|---|---|
| End User & Workplace Chapter Lead | Reports desk performance and staffing risk; escalates budget and cross-domain policy decisions | Escalates To |
| Client Platform Architect | Jointly defines Tier-2/3 escalation boundaries and OS-level support handoff procedures | Collaborates |
| Endpoint Management Architect | Jointly defines escalation boundaries for device enrollment and compliance issues | Collaborates |
| Service Desk Senior Analyst | Receives escalation and staffing feedback; sets SLA targets and coaching priorities | Provides To |

## Typical Day-to-Day Activities

- Reviewing the desk's SLA dashboard and reallocating shift coverage to address an emerging backlog
- Meeting with the Client Platform Architect to revisit escalation criteria after a spike in miscategorized Tier-2 handoffs
- Running 1:1s and coaching sessions with Senior Analysts on team performance and career development
- Reviewing a proposed standard change with the Change / Release Manager for the desk's most common recurring request type
- Briefing the desk on a scheduled major software rollout so Analysts recognize expected ticket spikes rather than treating them as an outage
- Presenting monthly SLA/CSAT attainment and staffing risk to the End User & Workplace Chapter Lead
- Interviewing and calibrating candidates for open Analyst or Senior Analyst positions
- Auditing a sample of recent tickets for escalation accuracy and knowledge base gap identification

## Remote Work Considerations

- **Remote Eligibility:** Hybrid — staffing/coaching and cross-domain escalation negotiation benefit from regular in-person or video-first engagement; ticket and SLA management itself is fully tooling-based.
- **Collaboration Tools:** ITSM/ticketing platform, Microsoft Teams, workforce scheduling tooling, Power BI or ServiceNow Performance Analytics, knowledge base platform.
- **On-Site Requirements:** Regular on-site presence typical for shift-supervision, team coaching, and walk-up desk oversight where one exists.
- **Time Zone Flexibility:** Standard business hours with availability to cover shift-boundary handoffs; more flexible than the Analyst/Senior Analyst rota itself.
- **On-Call / Operational Demands:** On-call for escalation of major staffing or coverage failures (e.g., a shift going unstaffed) and available as a point of contact during declared major incidents affecting end-user services.
